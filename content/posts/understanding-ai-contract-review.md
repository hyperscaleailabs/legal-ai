For most SMBs, contract review is an afterthought — something that happens on a deadline, with counsel on the phone and a red pen flying through pages nobody fully read. The result: missed auto-renewal clauses, unnoticed unlimited liability provisions, and indemnification traps buried in Schedule C.

AI-assisted contract review changes that equation entirely.

## The Traditional Review Bottleneck

A typical inbound contract — an NDA, a vendor MSA, a SaaS subscription agreement — takes a qualified attorney 2–4 hours to review properly. Multiply that by the 30–50 contracts a growing startup processes per month, and you're looking at a five-figure monthly spend before you've negotiated a single clause.

Worse, time pressure degrades quality. When a deal is closing and legal is underwater, reviewers triage rather than analyze. Critical provisions go unread.

![A lawyer reviewing stacks of paper contracts at a desk](/static/images/blog/lawyer-desk-inline.png)

## How AI Reads a Contract

Modern contract AI systems use large language models trained on millions of commercial agreements. When you upload a contract, the model:

1. **Segments the document** into individual clauses and provisions
2. **Classifies each clause** against a taxonomy (indemnification, limitation of liability, IP assignment, governing law, etc.)
3. **Evaluates risk** by comparing clause language against market-standard baselines
4. **Generates redlines** with plain-language rationale for every suggested change
5. **Produces a risk score** aggregated across all identified issues

The whole process takes under 60 seconds for a standard 15-page agreement.

## What AI Catches That Humans Miss

The value isn't just speed — it's consistency. A tired attorney skims; an AI never does. Common high-risk provisions that slip through manual review:

- **Uncapped indemnification** that puts your entire company on the hook for a counterparty's negligence
- **Auto-renewal with short cancellation windows** — often buried in Section 14 of a 20-page agreement
- **Unilateral amendment clauses** that let vendors change terms without notice
- **Overbroad IP assignment** that transfers ownership of anything built "in connection with" the engagement
- **Non-standard governing law** that would force you to litigate in a distant jurisdiction

## Where Humans Still Add Value

AI contract review is not a replacement for legal counsel — it's a triage layer. The goal is to eliminate the routine work so attorneys can focus on:

- **Novel provisions** that require judgment about business strategy, not just legal standards
- **Negotiation strategy** — what to push on, what to accept, where the counterparty has real leverage
- **Regulatory nuance** in highly regulated industries (healthcare, finance, defense)
- **Relationship context** — when to let something slide because the relationship is worth protecting

The best implementations use AI to flag and score, then route contracts above a risk threshold to a human reviewer with full context already assembled.

## A Before/After View

## Getting Started

If you're processing more than ten contracts per month and relying entirely on manual review, the ROI calculation is straightforward: one missed auto-renewal clause in a six-figure SaaS contract costs more than a year of AI-assisted review.

The question isn't whether AI belongs in your contract workflow. It's how quickly you can get it in there.
