The CFO asks legal how much contract review costs. Legal says: "About $450 per contract, maybe $180k last year." The CFO nods. The conversation ends.

That number is wrong — not because it's inaccurate, but because it only counts the visible costs. The real cost of manual contract review is three to five times higher when you include what most organizations never measure.

## The Visible Cost: Attorney Time

The $450 number comes from a simple calculation: average contract review time (2–3 hours) multiplied by effective attorney rate ($150–$200/hour for in-house, $350–$500 for outside counsel). Multiply by volume and you have a line item.

This is real money. A 200-person company processing 400 contracts per year at $450 each is spending $180,000 annually. For a startup using outside counsel at higher rates, the number doubles.

But attorney time is the cost everyone can see. It's the iceberg above water.

## The Hidden Cost: Deal Velocity

Every contract waiting in an attorney's queue is a deal not closing. The average contract review queue is 3–5 business days for in-house counsel during normal periods — and 10–15 days when volume spikes.

![A sales team waiting in a meeting room with a contract on the table](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1600880292203-757bb62b4baf?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

Consider what's in that queue:
- Vendor agreements blocking a product launch
- Customer contracts stalling a deal your sales team has been working for 3 months
- Partnership agreements that go cold while waiting for legal sign-off

The cost of a lost deal is hard to measure exactly — but the impact of routine delays on win rates is real. Sales teams develop workarounds: they push for verbal agreements, they start work before contracts are signed, they manage expectations by padding timelines. Each workaround introduces its own risk.

**Conservative estimate:** If delayed contracts cost your company 2–3 deal slippages per year at an average deal value of $40k, that's $80–$120k in lost or delayed revenue — invisible on the legal budget, very visible on the revenue forecast.

## The Hidden Cost: Inconsistent Review Quality

Manual review quality varies. It varies by reviewer, by workload, by time of day, by whether it's Tuesday before a holiday or Thursday at 9am. A contract reviewed in 45 minutes under deadline pressure is not the same as one reviewed over 3 hours with full focus.

The downstream effect: risk is unevenly distributed across your contract portfolio. Some agreements have been thoroughly reviewed; others have been skimmed. You don't know which is which.

This inconsistency creates two problems:

**1. Silent risk accumulation.** Clauses that would have been flagged in a careful review slip through during busy periods. These clauses sit in executed agreements for 2–3 year terms, accruing liability. The risk isn't visible until something goes wrong.

**2. No institutional memory.** Manual reviews don't produce structured, queryable data. When you want to know "how many of our contracts have uncapped indemnification?" — you can't answer without reading every contract again.

## The Hidden Cost: Reviewer Burnout and Turnover

Contract review is cognitively demanding and often tedious. Attorneys trained to litigate complex cases spend hours reviewing routine NDAs. Burnout is real and consequential.

When a legal team member leaves, their contract knowledge leaves with them. The internal understanding of which vendors have problematic terms, which customers have carve-outs, which agreements have unusual provisions — this institutional knowledge is rarely documented and rarely transferred.

Replacing a mid-level in-house attorney costs $100–$150k in recruiting fees and ramp-up time, plus 3–6 months of reduced team capacity. Burnout from high-volume routine work is a measurable contributor to turnover.

## What AI-Assisted Review Actually Changes

AI doesn't eliminate attorney judgment — it eliminates the routine work that precedes it. The economics shift:

| Cost Type | Manual Review | AI-Assisted |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Attorney time | 2–3 hours/contract | 20–30 min/contract (review only) |
| Review queue | 3–10 days | < 1 hour to triage |
| Consistency | Variable | Uniform baseline |
| Institutional memory | Informal, person-dependent | Structured, queryable |
| Reviewer burnout risk | High | Lower (focus on complex work) |

The goal isn't a cheaper legal function — it's a faster, more consistent one that scales without proportional headcount.

## Calculating Your Real Number

A more honest cost model:

1. **Direct attorney time** × volume × rate = visible cost
2. **Estimated deal slippage** from review delays = velocity cost
3. **Risk exposure** from inconsistent review = latent cost (estimate 0.5–1% of contract value portfolio)
4. **Turnover contribution** from routine review burden = talent cost

For most growing companies, the sum is 3–5x the visible attorney time. The business case for AI-assisted review isn't marginal — it's substantial.

The question is whether your legal team has the bandwidth to evaluate and implement it. That's the last irony of manual review: being too busy doing it to fix it.
